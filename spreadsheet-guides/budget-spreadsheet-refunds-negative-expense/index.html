How to Record Refunds in a Budget Spreadsheet (Without Inflating Your Income)

You earned $3,000, bought $50 of shoes, returned them, and now your budget says you earned $3,050. That happens when a refund goes in as income because "money came into the account". This checklist shows the usual fix: record the refund as a negative expense in the same category. It also covers the one case where that fix causes its own problem (refunds that land in a later month), and the formulas that make a spreadsheet handle it.

The formulas use SUMIFS and EOMONTH, which exist in Google Sheets, Microsoft Excel and LibreOffice Calc.

The short answer

Money that comes back Record it as Why
Store refund or return Negative expense, same category as the purchase It cancels spending; it isn't new income
A friend paying back their share Negative expense, same category Your real cost was only your share
Rebate or cashback tied to one purchase Negative expense, same category It lowers what that purchase cost you
Work reimbursement Either a negative expense, or a separate pair of categories that cancel out (see step 5) Keeps it out of both income and normal spending
Salary, interest, gifts, side income Income This is new money

This is a common budgeting convention, not an accounting rule. Personal budgets don't have to follow a standard, but it helps to pick one approach and use it every time.

The layout these formulas assume

A Transactions sheet with one row per transaction:

A: Date B: Description C: Type D: Category E: Amount
2026-10-01 Salary Income 3000
2026-10-03 Shoes Expense Clothing 50
2026-10-09 Shoes returned Expense Clothing -50

A Month sheet with the first day of the month in B1 (a real date) and one category per row from A7 down, with its budget in column B.

☐ 1. Keep the original purchase

Don't delete the purchase when you get the money back. Add a second row with the same category and a negative amount. The record stays true (you did buy and return the shoes), and the category nets to zero.

☐ 2. Enter the refund as Type = Expense with a minus sign

The refund row stays an Expense row. Only the sign changes. If you mark it as Income instead, your income total goes up and the category's spending stays where it was. That is the $3,050 problem.

☐ 3. Sum spending per category for the month

In the Month sheet, C7 (spent in the category named in A7):

=SUMIFS(Transactions!$E$2:$E$400,Transactions!$C$2:$C$400,"Expense",Transactions!$D$2:$D$400,A7,Transactions!$A$2:$A$400,">="&$B$1,Transactions!$A$2:$A$400,"<="&EOMONTH($B$1,0))

SUMIFS adds the amounts, so a −50 row lowers the total by 50. Fill it down for each category. Then:

  • Left in D7: =B7-C7
  • Over budget flag in E7: =IF(C7>B7,"Over budget","")

Month totals:

  • Income in B2: =SUMIFS(Transactions!$E$2:$E$400,Transactions!$C$2:$C$400,"Income",Transactions!$A$2:$A$400,">="&$B$1,Transactions!$A$2:$A$400,"<="&EOMONTH($B$1,0))
  • Expenses in B3: the same formula with "Expense"
  • Balance in B4: =B2-B3

With the three example rows above, income is 3,000, Clothing spending is 0, and the balance is 3,000.

Sources: SUMIFS (Google), SUMIFS (Microsoft), EOMONTH (Google).

☐ 4. Decide what to do when the refund lands in a later month

This is where most people get stuck. Say you bought an $80 jacket on 28 September and the refund arrived on 2 October:

  • Date the refund when it arrived (2 October). September shows Clothing +80, and October shows Clothing −80, which can make October's Clothing spending negative. Each month matches your bank account, but both months look odd.
  • Date the refund in the month of the purchase. September nets to 0 and October is untouched. Category totals are now right, but September's balance no longer matches what actually happened in your account that month.
  • Hybrid. Some people use a negative expense when the refund comes in the same month. For a later month, they use a "Refunds" income category plus a "Refunded" expense category, which they can include or exclude in reports.

None of these is wrong. Pick the one that matches what you use the budget for: tracking cash flow (date it when it arrived) or tracking category habits (date it with the purchase). Then stick to it.

☐ 5. Handle work reimbursements separately

If you pay work costs and get them back later, a separate Reimbursable expense category keeps them out of your normal spending. Pair it with a Reimbursement income category. Over time the two should cancel out. A gap tells you a reimbursement hasn't arrived yet.

☐ 6. Check that the totals still add up

  • Rows with no category. Leave the Category blank on an expense and it drops out of every category row, but it still counts in the month's Expenses. Add an "Uncategorized" line, Expenses − sum of all category rows, so the gap is visible.
  • Rows with no Type. A row with neither Income nor Expense in column C is counted nowhere. That's useful for notes, but it's easy to miss a real transaction this way.
  • Dates stored as text. These fall outside every date range. Check a suspicious row with =ISNUMBER(A2), which returns TRUE for a real date in Google Sheets, Excel and LibreOffice, since dates are stored as numbers.
  • The range is too short. If you have more than 399 transactions, extend $2:$400 in every formula.

☐ 7. Quick test before you rely on it

Add these rows in a copy of your sheet and check the Month view:

Rows Expect
Expense, Clothing, 50, then −50 in the same month Clothing spent = 0; income unchanged
Expense, Food, 120, then −25 (friend paid back) with a Food budget of 80 Food spent = 95, "Over budget"
Income 3,000 Income = 3,000 (not 3,050)
A row with Type left blank Not counted in income or expenses

  • Refunds: entered as negative amounts, they reduce that category's spending.
  • Categories: up to 12 expense categories, each with a monthly budget.
  • Transactions: Income/Expense and category dropdowns that reject values not on the list.
  • Month view (any month you pick): budget, spent and left per category, plus an "Over budget" flag. Expenses without a category get their own line, so totals always add up. Rows with no Type are not counted as income or expense.
