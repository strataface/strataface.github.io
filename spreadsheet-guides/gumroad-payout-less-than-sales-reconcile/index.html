Gumroad Payout Lower Than Your Sales? How to Reconcile It Line by Line

Your Gumroad dashboard says you sold $800 this month, and the payout is a lot less, or part of the balance seems to have vanished overnight. It's rarely a mistake. A payout is a different number from "sales", for a handful of documented reasons. This page shows how to reconcile one payout against your sales CSV line by line, then explains each adjustment so you know which column or dashboard line to check.

All rules below are from Gumroad's Help Center as of October 2026. Gumroad changes them occasionally, so check the linked pages if a number looks different.

Quick reference: what sits between "sales" and "payout"

Reconcile one payout, line by line

  1. Download the payout CSV. "You can download a CSV report for each payout from the Payouts dashboard, which includes a breakdown of all Gumroad fees".
  2. Export the sales CSV for the payout's date range, plus one day on each side.
  3. Keep only rows inside the window. Purchase Date is after the previous payout's cutoff and up to this payout's Friday cutoff (UTC).
  4. Remove PayPal Connect sales (check 3). They were paid when the sale happened.
  5. Sum Net Total ($) for the remaining rows.
  6. Subtract refunds and lost disputes charged in this window, and affiliate commissions. Add or subtract credits shown on the payout.
  7. Paid to PayPal? Subtract the 2% payout fee.
  8. Compare with the payout, then compare the payout with what actually arrived in your bank or PayPal account.

What each adjustment means

☐ 1. Compare against net, not gross

Gumroad's fees page: "For sales made on Gumroad's website, we charge a 10% + $0.50 fee + sales tax per transaction." That excludes "Credit card processing (2.9% + $0.30)" and "PayPal fees". Sales through Gumroad's Discover marketplace pay "a flat 30% fee, which includes all processing fees."

The sales CSV has the per-sale numbers: Sale Price ($), Fees ($) and Net Total ($) ("sale price - Gumroad's fees"). Add up Net Total ($), not Sale Price ($), when you compare with a payout. A Discover? column of 1 marks the 30% sales.

Note: the Help Center doesn't say whether card processing is inside Fees ($) for every sale type. Compare one sale's CSV row with the same sale in the payout breakdown before relying on it.

☐ 2. Use the right payout window

For reconciling, you only need the window. "Payouts include sales made through the previous Friday (UTC). On the weekly, monthly, and quarterly schedules, a sale has a minimum 7-day holding period" (daily and instant payouts, available only to some US accounts, don't wait 7 days). So filter your sales CSV by that window, not by the payout date. Sales after the cutoff belong to the next payout.

☐ 3. Take out sales that went straight to your PayPal

If you connected your PayPal account with Gumroad, PayPal sales are "credited to your connected account at the time of sale". On the payouts dashboard that amount appears as "PayPal Payouts" and "is subtracted from your total sales because it has already been paid out." Those sales show up in PayPal, not in the Gumroad payout. The sales CSV marks them in Payment Type, with a PayPal Transaction ID and PayPal Fee Amount.

☐ 4. Account for refunds, chargebacks and negative balances

  • On a refund, "Gumroad's own fee on the refunded portion is returned to you; only the underlying payment-processing portion of the fee is retained". Sales through your own connected Stripe or PayPal work differently.
  • Sales CSV columns: Refunded?, Partial Refund ($), Fully Refunded?, Disputed?, Dispute won?.
  • Note: the Help Center doesn't say exactly when a refund on an older sale is deducted. If your balance drops with no new sales, look for refunds or chargebacks on older sales first.
  • If the balance goes negative, Gumroad's payment processor "may debit your bank account".

☐ 5. Look at credits and affiliate commissions

Credits are "one-off financial adjustments" that "may be positive or negative": for example a won chargeback, VAT refunds or Australian back-taxes. They "are included as part of your weekly payouts". Affiliate commissions appear in the CSV as Affiliate commission ($) and "show up in your balance page as negative credits".

☐ 6. Paid to PayPal? Subtract the 2% payout fee

"PayPal payouts have a 2% payout fee", and "All PayPal payouts are processed in USD". This is separate from PayPal's fees on sales (check 3). For bank payouts in local currency, "currency conversions happen based on the exchange rates at the time of sale, not at the time of the payout", so the deposit won't match today's exchange rate.

☐ 7. Check the minimum and the account status

"You need a minimum balance of $100 USD to receive a payout" (some countries have higher local minimums). Below that, the balance carries over. If a payout is skipped entirely rather than smaller, read Gumroad's payout-delays article and check your payout settings.

☐ 8. Mind the time zone

The payouts page "is synced to UTC" while the rest of the dashboard uses your account's time zone, so totals for "the same period" can differ slightly. The sales CSV is in UTC too. Gumroad suggests adding "a one-day buffer to the beginning and end of your date range" when you export.

Symptom table

Symptom Check

If you sell on more than one platform

Doing this every week for Gumroad, plus Lemon Squeezy and Payhip, gets old. Solo OS ($29, Google Sheets) is a finance sheet for one-person online sellers. According to its product page, you paste your Gumroad, Lemon Squeezy and Payhip sales exports, and columns are matched by header name. It then deducts platform fees, affiliate commissions, PayPal/Stripe Connect fees, refunds and lost disputes to get net income, and converts it to your home currency with default or monthly FX rates. Its payout reconciliation matches PayPal and Payoneer deposits against what each platform owes you and marks each platform OK, PENDING or CHECK. If Gumroad pays you to a bank account, that deposit matching won't see it. It also has a monthly dashboard and tax-form reminders, and an English/Chinese interface. Its listed limits: not tax advice; 500 rows per platform tab by default; and the sample CSVs' headers are built from each platform's documentation, not checked against real seller exports, so rename headers if yours differ.
